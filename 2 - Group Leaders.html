Mission + Mantras

Who We Are

As members of the body of Christ, we seek Great Commission and Great Commandment living (Matt. 22:37–40 · Matt. 28:18–20).

As members of this local body of Christ called Southeast, we seek to live this way through connecting people to Jesus and one another, utilizing some metaphors based on the life of Christ to keep us focused.

You, as a Group Leader in Student Ministry, are a culture carrier of our Mission and Mantras. You should frequently use them as a GPS to keep our ministry efforts focused on accomplishing Great Commission and Great Commandment living.

Small Group Leader Expectations

What We Ask of You

Five expectations, one for each of our mantras—the pictures from the life of Christ that shape how we lead.

Be the BranchJohn 15:1–17
We connect in community. We value connection to Jesus over production for Jesus.
  • Attend a weekend service.
  • Be in a group of your own—Bible study, small group, etc.
  • Have, or be seeking, a personal mentor.
  • Be growing in your personal study and prayer disciplines.
Catch the WindJohn 3:1–8
We connect the full force of the church with the Holy Spirit. We value risk and adventure over safety and comfort.
  • Build the boat well. Come prepared: 30 minutes in the week’s passage, writing questions, and praying for your group.
  • Work with the wind. Ask questions. Talk no more than 20% of group time.
  • Know your crew. Know how to push each student, when to give them their moment, and when to redirect.
The Basics
Co-leaders + teams
Every group has at least one co-leader, on a larger team in your grade and ministry.
 
On time
30 minutes before service, every week.
 
August to July
Students thrive on consistency. Your co-leader covers you for sickness or travel.

Small Group Leader Expectations
Wreck the RoofLuke 5:18–25
We will radically love people one at a time. We value innovation over tradition.
  • Create new ways to connect with your group—hang-outs, bringing things to group.
  • Lead your group to invite friends. Plan how you will celebrate a new student, and create a group identity.
  • Know where each student is spiritually. Anyone not baptized? Anyone struggling with faith?
  • Have a Timothy. When asked, be able to say their name. Do for one what you wish you could do for everyone.
Grip the PlowLuke 9:57–62
We serve with intentional and focused effort. We value personal engagement over attendance.
  • Serve with HSM or MSM every week, including the leader meeting.
  • Have one touchpoint with a student outside of service each week—a text, a shared scripture, coffee, a game or recital.
Empty the JarLuke 7:36–50
We connect through sacrificial generosity. We value expressive worship over religious rituals.
  • Go to the big events: Camp (HSC / MSC), Experience WKND (MSM overnight), Fall Retreat (HSM overnight).
  • Own a +1. Step up and own one more responsibility: communion prep, set-up and tear-down, hosting, teaching, tech, contacting first-time guests.

Leading Well

Before Group Starts

Most group problems are solved before group starts.

01
Sit with your students
During large group, spread out through the room, not along the back wall. It protects the environment, builds shared memories, and keeps you close when a student needs you.
02
Split up the leaders
Sit across the circle from your co-leader. You’ll engage the whole group and cut down on side conversations.
03
Set group guidelines
Let students help write them, then come back to them all semester. Start with these:
Respect one anotherStay with the group the whole timeWhat’s shared here stays hereDon’t interruptPut away distractions (like phones)
Leading Well in Groups16
Leading a Group

5 Keys

01
Create an environment for discussion
Sit in a circle, on the same level. Everyone can see each other—and everyone feels free to share.
02
Turn statements into questions
Facilitate, don’t teach. Use more question marks than periods, and skip yes-or-no questions.
03
Involve every student
Don’t fill the silence with your own answer. Ask “What do you think?” and give it room—a little awkward silence never hurt anybody.
04
Don’t read off the curriculum
Know the topic ahead of time and ask in your own words. The less you look down, the more it feels like a conversation.
05
Follow up during the week
Students need someone to walk with them, not talk at them. See Follow Up for how—page 23.
Leading Well in Groups17
When a Group Stalls

Getting Unstuck

01
Get easy wins
Blank stares usually mean the question was too hard. Ask simpler questions to build confidence, then work toward tougher ones.
02
Get fun
The first part of group is for fun, so students grow as a family. Use that time to earn the right to challenge them.
03
Get out
Experiences beyond Sunday—serving and showing up during the week—directly fuel your group time.
04
Get better questions
A great question yields as much as a great answer. Practice them before group; write a few of your own.
05
Get visual
A big sticky pad and a few markers pull everyone in. Drawing or writing answers makes the lesson stick.
Leading Well in Groups18
Leading Well

Prayer in Groups

Your group may be the only place a student is prayed for all week.

01
Ask for requests
“How can I pray for you?” Then go further: what are you thankful for? Where have you seen God move?
02
Write them down
What students share matters. It’s okay to pull out paper—it helps you keep praying all week.
03
Set time aside
Don’t save prayer for the last minute. Rushed prayer tells students it isn’t important.
04
Let students pray
Ask who wants to pray for the group. If no one volunteers, happily take it yourself.
05
Tell them you’re praying
Remind students you pray for them during the week, and check in on the hard ones.
Leading Well in Groups19
Leading Well

Student Behavior

The goal is to keep every student in the room. Start with your group guidelines (page 16). When behavior keeps getting in the way, follow three strikes.

01
Strike 1
Both leaders talk with the student
Have an intentional conversation: “We’re so glad you’re here, and when you’re here, you can’t ______.” Name the specific behavior with examples, and make sure the student understands.
02
Strike 2
Bring in your team leader
A second conversation addresses the issue with the next level of leadership.
03
Strike 3
Bring in staff
The student is told that if the issue isn’t corrected, staff will call a meeting with their parents or guardians.
Every campus has different layers of leadership. If your campus doesn’t have a team leader in place, go to staff at Strike 2. A safety concern is never a strike—bring in staff right away (page 34).

Shepherding Students

Earning Trust

Students open up to leaders they trust. Five ways to earn it:

01
Learn their name
A name is the sweetest sound to a student. Learning it says they’re known, loved, and important.
02
Be consistent
When students can count on you being there, they learn they can count on you.
03
Show up when you don’t have to
Games, recitals, events. Time outside of programming builds the relationship in a more carefree setting.
04
Jump into teachable moments
Everyday moments open doors to talk about Jesus. Step through them.
05
Speak truth in love
Hard truths land best in a loving, non-threatening space.Ephesians 4:15
Shepherding Beyond Groups22
Shepherding Students

Follow Up

Shepherding students well means listening well in group—and following up after it.

01
Listen for the follow-up
A test, a tryout, a hard week at home. When a student shares it in group, that’s your next conversation.
02
Ask the next question
“How did it go?” opens the door. “What was the hardest part?” keeps it open.
03
Check in during the week
A text, a card, a quick call. Keep group texts plural and include your co-leader—see Digital Guardrails, page 36.
04
Close the loop
If something is heavy, ask for updates and let them know you’re still praying. If it’s a yellow or red light, bring in staff—page 34.

Five things a new believer needs to grow before becoming a worker—ask how Jesus shaped each in His disciples.

The Bridge

1
Us + God

Genesis 1 describes the start of everything, including the start of humanity. God made us and began a relationship with us.

2

Genesis 3 describes how our relationship with God was broken by humanity’s disobedience.

We call this disobedience “sin.” God can have nothing to do with sin. Read Romans 3:23.

3

Romans 6:23 and Isaiah 59:2 describe the problem sin creates and its consequences. Sin separates us from God and causes death. There is nothing we can do to return to God on our own.

4

Romans 5:8, Romans 10:9–10 and Acts 2:38 tell us about the solution. His name is Jesus.

Jesus died for everyone’s sin, conquered death, and made a way for us to be with God again. When we trust in Jesus and choose to be baptized, we are saved!

Safety & Care

Conversations Guide

SE Students wants every leader to promote safety and awareness in tough conversations. Ministry is messy and these topics can feel intimidating—here are the guidelines for how to best proceed.

Green Light
“Green means go.”
You’ve got this. You have both the Sword and the Spirit.
  • Family problems
  • Relational problems
  • Homesickness
  • Heartfelt confessions
  • Questions about dating & sex
  • Church & theological questions
Yellow Light
“Proceed with caution.”
Mention it to a staff member for coaching.
  • Bullying
  • Pornography
  • Eating disorders
  • Drugs or alcohol
  • Anxiety or depression
  • Engagement in sexual activity
  • Gender & sexual identity questions
Red Light
“Stop and get help.”
Get a staff member and bring them into the conversation.
  • Self-injury
  • Hearing voices / hallucinations
  • Sexual & physical abuse reported
  • Suicidal / homicidal thoughts (threats to harm)
Safety & Care34
Safety Guardrails

Student to Leader: In Person

Southeast takes seriously the responsibility of keeping our people safe.  Here are some best practices when interacting with students in person. 

Programming
•Keep students in programming. We want all students in the student room. If a student wants to talk one-on-one, wait until after program and groups end, and meet in a public area where you are visible to others.
•Appropriate interactions. Appropriate touches with a student: handshakes, high-fives, and side hugs.
•When a student's ride is late. Never leave a student waiting alone—wait with two adults. If you have a parent or guardian's permission to drive a student home, bring another cleared adult.
Counseling
•Counsel same gender. If a student of the opposite gender comes to you for counsel, connect them with a leader of the same gender.
•Be visible. Meet in a public area where you are visible to other people. Never meet in private.
•Review the Counseling Spotlight. When counseling a student, refer to the Stoplight Guide on how to best proceed in conversation.
Outside of Church
•Pair up with another leader. When planning an outside activity with students, always include another cleared adult and stay within the 1:25 student/leader ratio.
•Transporting students. Never give a student a ride home unless you have another cleared leader in the vehicle and a parent's or guardian's approval.
•Communicating digitally. When texting or using social media with students, follow the Digital Communication Safety Guardrails on the next page.
Safety & Care35
Safety Guardrails

Student to Leader: Online

When it comes to digital interactions, the following are some best practices to promote safety and create a healthy environment between leaders and students. 

Talk to Parents First
Make parents and guardians fully aware of all the digital methods that will be used to communicate with students for ministry purposes.
Keep It Plural
Want to let an absent student know they were missed, or tell someone you're proud of them? Use plural pronouns instead of singular ones. Rather than “I missed you,” say “We missed you.”
Add a Co-Leader
Adding another leader helps with accountability. Group texting is the preference for all digital communication. At no time should one-on-one video communication take place between a student and leader.
Communication Limits
Most conversations can wait until the next day—a healthy boundary. Use “scheduled sending” for messages composed outside acceptable hours (e.g. write a message at 9:30 p.m. and schedule it to go out at 7:00 a.m. the next day).
Traceable Communication
Apps with a “self-destruct mode” can create dangers when adults use them to communicate with minors. To be above reproach, only communicate with students in ways that are traceable. Never delete a conversation with a student.

Big Three41
Preceding Each of the Big Three

Unleashed Leaders (All Year)

Unleashed Leaders is a deep dive into our “why”—a training we expect every new leader to go through.

It sets up new leaders to learn who we are as a Student Ministry. Whether someone joins to lead a group or serve at check-in, they need to know our primary aim is to make disciples. We look at the life of Christ and allow it to inform how we approach ministry.

Since Jesus himself continually raised the bar for his disciples, we inform those joining us about the cost of following Jesus up front, so later they aren’t surprised by what we ask of them. In some ways it can appear we are talking people out of doing ministry. In actuality, we are just echoing Jesus’ call to make disciples—even some who heard it straight from Jesus’ mouth chose not to follow.

We know things happen—but being up front about the commitment pays dividends when leaders know what they are signing up for and stick it out. Know someone who should serve with us? Unleashed Leaders is their front door—invite them and point them to the next one. We love the community of leaders God has blessed us with. We get to fulfill Jesus’ mission together!

The Mission

Four Questions

Connecting students to Jesus and one another starts with four questions for every leader:

01
How am I connecting to Jesus?
Your connection to Christ matters more than what you offer in service to Christ. God’s Word and prayer are the fuel for an effective ministry.
02
How am I connecting to one another?
Being connected to Jesus isn’t enough. Jesus modeled community through the Trinity, but also through the disciples he walked with. Remember, ministry is a team effort.
03
How am I connecting people to Jesus?
As we are transformed, our purpose shifts. Jesus understood His mission—to seek and save the lost. He left the mission in the disciples’ hands. That mission is now yours to carry.
04
How am I connecting people to one another?
Neighborhoods, schools, workplaces and families need you to have vision for cultivating community. Belonging often opens the door of reception to Christ.

Now & Next Values

It’s easy to focus on what’s happening in a student’s life now and forget to prepare them for what’s next. These five values shape how we lead—and what we ask of you.

01
Be equipped
You’re not on your own. We’ll equip and empower you to shepherd students through training, huddles, and a team leader in your corner.Ephesians 4:12
02
Partner with parents
Parents are the biggest influence in a student’s faith. Learn their names, keep them in the loop, and work with them, not around them.
03
Reach the lost
Help your students see their friends the way Jesus does—and give them the courage to invite and share.
04
Next steps
Help every student take their next step with Jesus and in life: baptism, a group, a serving role, a mission trip.
05
Intergenerational
Students grow when adults of every age know them. Connect them to the wider church to serve, worship, and learn alongside other generations.
